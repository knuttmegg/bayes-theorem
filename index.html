Bayes' theorem

Posterior odds equal prior odds times the likelihood ratio, raised to the number of independent observations: O(H|E) = O(H) × λⁿ. Probability is P = O / (1 + O). The map from prior to posterior is concave for confirming evidence (λ > 1) and convex for disconfirming evidence (λ < 1).

Why the curve bends

Write f(p) = λp / (λp + 1 − p). The second derivative has the sign of 1 − λ. Confirming evidence (λ > 1) is concave in probability: high priors sit on the flat top, so extra confirmation barely moves P(H|E). Disconfirming evidence (λ < 1) is convex: the bow below the diagonal is why a 75% belief can collapse under a few contrary observations even though each observation has a fixed bit-weight.

Thinking in log-odds restores linearity: each independent observation adds n log₂ λ bits. The sliders separate the direction of the evidence (λ) from how much of it you have (n).
